Two verbatim templates Justin uses for LinkedIn outreach. Match this voice and structure when drafting; fill the bracketed slots. Keep consistent with [[feedback_outreach_style_rules]], [[feedback_outreach_ask_type]], [[feedback_outreach_research]], [[feedback_outreach_reengagement_and_seniority]].

## 1. Connection note (referral hook, ~300 char limit)
> Hi [First], [Referrer] suggested I reach out to you. I'm Justin, an MS Computer Engineering graduate from RIT working on robotics, and edge AI projects. Would love to connect and learn from your journey. Thanks!

Structure: greeting + referral line + name/degree opener + one-line focus (robotics, edge AI) + soft connect ask. Drop the referral line when there is no referrer.

## 2. First message after they accept (warm intro -> conversation ask)
> Hi [First], I am glad we could connect.
>
> I graduated this month and am currently looking for jobs in the US looking forward to building a career in robotics be it embedded, AI or GPU optimization I am equally inclined towards them.
> I don't have any full-time experience only a few internships and currently in the application process focussing on edge AI and model optimization roles.
> I needed some guidance on what skills are required and what expectations companies have from new grads. Given your career at [Company] as a [Their Role] I was wondering if we could have a small conversation about this if your schedule allows, as I strongly believe your experience and insights could really help me understand the current trends.
> I really look forward to this conversation.

Ask type: guidance / short conversation ("small conversation"), NOT a resume or referral ask up front. Personalize the "[Company] as a [Their Role]" clause to the recipient.

Notes when reusing:
- "graduated this month" is time-bound — update to "graduated in May 2026" now that it is past (see [[feedback_grad_date_language]]).
- The message has a mild run-on and restates the ask twice ("small conversation" + "look forward to this conversation"); [[feedback_outreach_ask_type]] flags both. Offer to tighten unless Justin wants it verbatim.

## 3. Direct referral-ask system (finalized 2026-08-26) — two branches

This is a separate, more direct family from templates 1-2 above: it names the target role/company explicitly and asks for a referral up front instead of leading with a guidance/conversation ask. It also permits "6 internships" as a stated exception to the no-numbers rule (Justin's explicit call, see [[feedback_outreach_style_rules]]). Use this family when there's a specific open role to point at; use templates 1-2 when there isn't.

**Branch A — send connection request WITH a note** (kept under 300 chars for LinkedIn's note field):
> Hi [Name], I'm Justin, an embedded firmware and edge AI engineer with a robotics background and 6 internships. Saw an opening for [Role] at [Company] that fits my profile well. Would love to connect and, if it works out, get a referral for the role.

Then once they accept, send:
> Thank you for accepting the connection request. As mentioned above, I'm still interested in this role since it lines up well with my background. Could you refer me to the team for this position?
>
> Job Link: [url]

**Branch B — send connection request WITHOUT a note**, then once accepted send a full message:
> Hello [Name],
>
> I am Justin and I have an M.S. in Computer Engineering from Rochester Institute of Technology, with a background in embedded systems, machine vision, and robotics. [Company]'s work lines up closely with the kind of engineering I want to be doing, and your background in [their relevant area] was useful context before reaching out.
>
> I wanted to learn more about the [team] team at [Company], since I am looking to apply there.
>
> I would appreciate the chance to connect further and hear your perspective. Thank you for your time.

Fixes already applied so don't reintroduce: the connection note was originally ~340 chars (over the 300 cap) before trimming; the accept message originally restated "refer me... or put in a word" (two asks for one thing) and used "Job Link :-" (dash as punctuation, banned by rule 1); Branch B originally repeated the company name twice in one sentence with a redundant "wanted... was wanting" clause, and was missing a space between "perspective." and "Thank you."
