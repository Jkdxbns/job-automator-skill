This file contains Justin's actual example outreach messages. These define the
exact tone, structure, and length to replicate. Do not deviate from this style.

## Cold Email Example (Full Length)

**To:** Gilbert (recruiter/engineer at MatX)
**Role:** Kernel Engineer at MatX

```
Hi Gilbert,

I am Justin Mascarenhas. I have an MS in Computer Engineering from Rochester 
Institute of Technology, NY. I wanted to reach out about MatX's Kernel Engineer 
opening. My background is in Computer Engineering with hands on work across CUDA 
optimization, edge AI deployment, embedded C and C++, and transformer model 
efficiency, and it lines up well with MatX's hardware aware software stack.

Most relevant to this role, I rewrote CUDA convolution kernels for VGG16 inference so 
memory access patterns were more efficient, which brought a solid speedup in the 
convolution step. I also worked on edge deployment constraints through pruning and 
quantization of a small language model, which cut down memory footprint by a good 
margin, and I deployed a TensorRT based YOLO tracker on Jetson for a real time robotic 
control pipeline.

Beyond ML inference, I have low level embedded experience. I've programmed STM32 
peripherals directly in bare metal C, including GPIO, timers, ADC, and I2C, and got 
the firmware footprint down quite small. I'm interested in MatX because the role sits 
right at the intersection of specialized hardware, ML workloads, and performance 
critical systems software. Your recruiting background in developer roles also made 
you a good person to reach out to directly.

I'd be glad to connect and talk about how my CUDA, C and C++, embedded systems, and 
edge ML optimization experience could be useful for MatX's compute platform work. 
I've attached my resume.

Best,
Justin Mascarenhas
justinmascarenhas.com
```

## Style Analysis (Extracted Rules)

### Email structure:
1. **Para 1 — One line who I am, then why reaching out:** Always start with a plain one liner naming Justin and his M.S. in Computer Engineering (see the one liner rule below), then the role being applied to and one sentence connecting background to the company's stack/mission or, if a real hook exists (see the hook rule below), lead the hook here instead.
2. **Para 2 — Technical proof, described qualitatively:** 2–3 specific technical achievements from the tailored resume, described in plain terms (what it was, what it solved, roughly how well it worked) without citing the exact numbers from the resume. State fit against the JD's stated requirements explicitly rather than leaving it implied.
3. **Para 3 — The ask:** See the ask rule below for choosing between a conversation ask and a document ask. Exactly one ask, stated once, no restating the same request a second way in the same sentence or paragraph.
4. **Sign-off:** "Best, Justin Mascarenhas" + the portfolio root `justinmascarenhas.com`, exactly as
   fixed in the sign-off rule. Not a LinkedIn URL and not a `/embedded` or `/edge-ai` track page.

Note: the 4 paragraph "breadth + hook, then separate ask paragraph" structure from the
original MatX example above is fine for engineer/technical targets, but for recruiter
targets prefer folding the hook into paragraph 1 (see the Diana/Sandisk example) and
going straight to a single ask paragraph — shorter is better per the length rule below.

### LinkedIn DM structure:
1. **Para 1 — One line who I am, then the role:** Same one liner rule as email, then spotted the role.
2. **Para 2 — Fit:** 2–3 relevant areas described qualitatively (DMs are shorter and never needed metrics anyway).
3. **Para 3 — Ask:** Share resume + brief connect. Explicit referral ask phrased as "if my background seems like a fit" (not demanding).
4. **Closing:** "Thank you so much in advance."

### The one liner rule:
Every email and DM opens with a short, plain sentence identifying Justin and his degree, before anything else. Default form: "I have an M.S. in Computer Engineering from Rochester Institute of Technology." Only attach a specific date (e.g. "graduated May 2026" or "graduating May 2026", tense checked against the current date, see [[feedback_grad_date_language]]) when the target role is explicitly grad date bound, like a "New College Grad" posting with a stated eligibility window. Otherwise leave the date out entirely — it's not needed and adds a number to flag under the no numbers rule.

### Tone rules:
- Formal but direct — no filler phrases
- **Never use:** "I hope this finds you well", "I came across your profile" (LinkedIn DM uses it once — that's the exception), "passionate about", "leverage", "synergy", "reach out" (use "reach out" sparingly), "excited to", "love to" (ok sparingly)
- **Never use any hyphen or dash character, of any kind, anywhere in the email or DM** — not "-", not "--", not the en dash, not the em dash. This applies to subject, greeting, body, and sign-off. Rewrite instead of substituting: split into two sentences, use "and"/"or"/"which", use a comma, or drop the connecting clause entirely. Compound adjectives that would normally take a hyphen (e.g. "real-time", "bare-metal", "end-to-end") should be rewritten as plain phrases ("in real time", "at the bare metal level", "from start to finish") or left as a closed compound where natural ("realtime" is not standard — prefer rephrasing over inventing spellings).
- **Keep the language plain and a little uneven** — this is what makes it read as a person, not a generator. Vary sentence length noticeably (mix short, blunt sentences with longer ones). Avoid the "smooth AI cadence": no perfectly balanced three-part lists, no "not only X but also Y", no stacked adjectives, no overly tidy transitions ("Furthermore", "Moreover", "In addition"). Use plain connectors like "and", "also", "so", "but". Prefer everyday words over formal synonyms (say "used" not "utilized", "built" not "architected", "helped" not "facilitated") unless the formal word is the actual technical term.
- Use first person confidently — "I optimized", "I built", "I deployed"
- **No numbers in the body** — do not cite exact figures, percentages, sizes, or counts (no "1.5x speedup", no "3.68 KB", no "11 integration failures"). Describe the same achievements qualitatively instead: what the work was, what problem it solved, what the outcome was in plain terms. Numbers are fine in the resume itself — this rule is for outreach mail and DMs only.
- **No permission granting or forgiving language, especially in follow ups** — never write "no worries if it got buried", "no rush on your end", "totally understand if you're busy", or similar. These sound like Justin is excusing the recipient's delay from a position of authority he doesn't have; he's the one asking for a favor, not the one granting absolution. State the follow up directly and skip the reassurance line entirely.
- **Always open with a one line description of who you are**, naming yourself and that you hold (or are completing) an M.S. in Computer Engineering. Do not tie this to a specific month unless the target's role is explicitly recruiting for a defined grad-date window (e.g. a "New College Grad" req) — otherwise just "M.S. in Computer Engineering from Rochester Institute of Technology" is enough, no date needed.
- Company-specific: always name the company, the specific role, and a specific reason for reaching out to this person (their title, their recruiter background, a post they made, etc.)
- Email length: 120–170 words (recruiter response data shows shorter performs better, see [[feedback_outreach_research]])
- DM length: 120–180 words
- **No run on sentences.** If a sentence needs two separate thoughts (e.g. proposing a meeting AND stating the purpose of it), put a period or comma boundary between them. Read every draft back and check that no independent clause is glued to another with no punctuation at all.
- **Never state the same intent twice in one sentence or back to back sentences.** If the ask paragraph says "I'd like to talk" one way, don't also say "I would really like to talk through" right after it. Say it once, cleanly.

### The ask rule:
Exactly one ask per message, and it should match the target's role:
- **Recruiter, especially university/early career/mentor-type recruiters** (title includes "University Recruiter", "Early Careers", "Mentor", "Talent Development", or their profile emphasizes coaching/relationship building): prefer a **conversation ask** ("I would love a quick virtual coffee sometime, if you are open to it, to talk through how my background fits this role, whichever time works best for you") over a document ask. A conversation ask signals genuine interest and costs the recruiter nothing to say yes to; a bare "take a look at my resume" ask hands them a task instead. See the Diana/Sandisk example above for the template.
- **Technical peer / engineer / hiring manager:** a lighter touch works — offering to share the resume and connect briefly is fine, since their time to review technical fit is the actual bottleneck, not a conversation.
- **Never stack asks.** Pick the one ask that fits the target and drop the rest (don't also ask for a referral, also ask them to review the resume, also ask to connect, all in the same message).

### Calibrate technical depth to the target's role:
- **Engineering VP / Tech Lead / Peer engineer:** full technical depth — specific protocols, tools, frameworks, metrics
- **CEO / Co-Founder / CXO:** outcome-focused — what shipped, team size, reliability results. No register-level details, no protocol names. They care about: can this person build things, do they ship, what's the risk.
- **Operations / Field roles:** field reliability and cross-team collaboration focus. Translate firmware work into what it means for the system in the field, not how it works internally.

### What to personalize per target:
- Their first name in greeting
- Specific role title (exact from JD)
- Company name (exact)
- Why *this person* specifically (title relevance, recruiter background, engineering background, etc.)
- Which 2–3 technical achievements are most relevant to their role (pulled from tailored resume)
- Company-specific hook: why Justin is interested in *this company* specifically (product, mission, tech stack)
